What Is Delrin (Acetal)? Precision Machining and Low-Friction Applications

Among engineers who machine precision plastic components, Delrin has a reputation that is hard to argue with. It holds tighter tolerances than nylon, absorbs virtually no moisture, and machines with exceptional consistency.

What Is Delrin and How Does It Differ from Generic Acetal?

Delrin is DuPont's trade name for homopolymer acetal, a highly crystalline thermoplastic formally known as polyoxymethylene (POM). It is one of two primary acetal grades — the other being copolymer acetal (Celcon or Acetron GP). Homopolymer Delrin offers higher mechanical strength, better fatigue resistance, and a harder surface, making it the preferred grade for demanding machined components.

Delrin absorbs less than 0.25% moisture and maintains its dimensions with exceptional consistency across varying temperature and humidity conditions. For precision assemblies where tolerances are measured in thousandths of an inch, this property alone often determines the material specification.

02

What Are the Key Properties of Delrin?

Dimensional stability: Less than 0.25% moisture absorption — negligible dimensional change in varying humidity. The defining property where nylon would be unreliable.

Surface hardness: Harder than nylon or UHMW, producing better wear performance in sliding and rotating contact applications.

Low friction: Natural lubricity reduces friction in gear, cam, and bushing applications without requiring external lubrication.

Stiffness and creep resistance: Higher flexural modulus than nylon with excellent resistance to creep under sustained load.

Machinability: Machines exceptionally cleanly — sharp edges, fine surface finishes, consistent tolerances across production runs.

Chemical resistance: Resistant to fuels, oils, solvents, and most neutral chemicals. Less resistant to strong acids and oxidizing agents.

Temperature range: Continuous service to approximately 220°F (104°C).

One consideration: Delrin has a relatively high coefficient of thermal expansion compared to metals. For assemblies combining Delrin with metal components in temperature-cycling environments, design tolerances should accommodate differential expansion.

Gears and sprockets: Delrin's stiffness, surface hardness, and low friction make it the standard specification for precision gears in office equipment, medical devices, and industrial machinery.

Bushings and bearings: Dimensionally stable under load and in variable humidity — maintains fit and function where nylon bushings would loosen or tighten with moisture uptake.

Valve components: Valve seats, pump impellers, and manifold components where chemical resistance to fuels/oils, dimensional precision, and low friction are all required.

04

How Does Delrin Compare to Nylon?

The decision almost always comes down to dimensional stability and moisture sensitivity. Nylon absorbs up to 8% moisture by weight in some grades compared to less than 0.25% for Delrin — causing measurable dimensional growth and reduced stiffness in humid environments.

Choose Delrin When:

Tight tolerances must be maintained regardless of humidity. Dimensional stability and consistent fit are the primary requirements. Creep resistance under sustained load is needed.

Choose Nylon When:

Better impact resistance is needed. Parts must flex without fracturing. Oil-filled nylon is required for extended dry-running friction wear life.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Delrin and acetal?

Delrin is DuPont's trade name for homopolymer acetal (POM). Acetal is the broader category including copolymer grades (Celcon, Acetron GP). Homopolymer Delrin offers higher strength, better fatigue resistance, and a harder surface than copolymer acetal.

Why does Delrin hold tighter tolerances than nylon?

Delrin absorbs less than 0.25% moisture, so its dimensions remain stable in varying humidity. Nylon absorbs much more and grows dimensionally — making it unreliable for tight-tolerance assemblies across changing conditions.

Is Delrin suitable for food contact applications?

Natural (white) Delrin meets FDA requirements for incidental food contact in many applications. It is not NSF-certified for all food processing uses. For full NSF or USDA compliance, HDPE or UHMW are typically more straightforward specifications.

Can Delrin be used in outdoor or wet environments?

Delrin performs well in wet environments due to low moisture absorption. It has moderate UV resistance and will degrade slowly under prolonged direct sunlight without stabilization. For outdoor structural applications, black UV-stabilized HDPE is often more practical.
